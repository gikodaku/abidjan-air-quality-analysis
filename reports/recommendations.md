# Air quality in Abidjan: findings and recommendations

*Briefing note for health and environment authorities. PM2.5 measured by the US Embassy's
reference monitor (February 2020 – April 2025), and six pollutants from the Copernicus CAMS model
(August 2022 – October 2026). Method and limitations:
[technical documentation](../docs/technical_docs.md).*

## Key findings

**1. Fine-particle pollution is five times the WHO guideline.**
The US Embassy monitor measured an average PM2.5 of 25.1 µg/m³, five times the WHO annual
guideline (5 µg/m³). Three days in four (73%) exceed the daily guideline of 15 µg/m³, and about
one day in five (19%) is unhealthy for sensitive groups or worse.

**2. The harmattan is the critical period.**
From December to February the measured PM2.5 averages 42 µg/m³, against 18 µg/m³ in the rainy
season, and 99% of harmattan days exceed the WHO guideline. In the model data, PM10 exceeds its
daily guideline on 35% of harmattan days, and ozone exceeds its 8-hour guideline on 12% of them.
The worst episodes happen then: on 8 February 2024, the model showed PM2.5 at 126 µg/m³ and PM10
at 211 µg/m³, "Very unhealthy" for the whole population.

**3. Two sources dominate: Saharan dust and combustion.**
Northerly winds bring twice as many particles as southerly sea winds. All year round, PM2.5 moves
together with carbon monoxide (correlation 0.56), a marker of combustion: biomass fires, wood and
charcoal cooking, waste burning and traffic.

**4. There is no sign of improvement.**
The monitor's yearly averages were 25.7, 29.8, 22.4, 19.5, 27.8 and 29.4 µg/m³ from 2020 to 2025.
The model data seemed to show a fall after 2023, but the monitor does not: that fall came from a
change in the model, not from cleaner air.

**5. Pollution can be forecast several days ahead.**
The system forecasts six pollutants three days ahead and beats the "tomorrow = today" baseline for
five of them. Its PM2.5 is calibrated on the embassy monitor, which cuts the error by a quarter
compared with the raw model.

**6. Measurement has stopped.**
The US Embassy monitor, the only public reference station found for Abidjan, has not reported
since 1 May 2025. Without it, the forecasts can no longer be checked against reality.

**7. Traffic pollution is not visible in this data.**
The model shows almost no nitrogen dioxide (NO2), which is implausible for a city of 6 million
people. This is a limit of the data, not good news: only ground monitors can measure it.

## Recommendations

### Short term: warn the public
1. **Daily air-quality bulletin**, at least from December to February, broadcast by radio, social
   media and SMS from the D+1 to D+3 forecast. It should target schools, health centres and sports
   clubs first.
2. **Pollution episode protocol**, triggered when the forecast exceeds 35.5 µg/m³ of PM2.5
   ("Unhealthy for sensitive groups"):
   - postpone outdoor school sport;
   - alert respiratory and paediatric services;
   - advise people with asthma.

### Medium term: cut the controllable sources
3. **Open burning**: strengthen waste collection and the ban on burning, especially during the
   harmattan, when the air is already full of dust.
4. **Clean cooking**: support access to LPG and improved stoves for households and fish smokers,
   who are also the most exposed.
5. **Traffic**: emissions testing, renewal of public transport vehicles (gbakas, wôrô-wôrôs) and
   continued mass-transit projects (metro, BRT).

### Medium term: measure
6. **Restore a public reference station**, since the embassy monitor has stopped.
7. **Ground sensor network** in several districts with different profiles (Plateau, Yopougon,
   Abobo, Treichville, Cocody, Port-Bouët): calibrated low-cost sensors measuring PM2.5, PM10 and
   NO2. Without them, local pollution and differences between neighbourhoods stay invisible, and
   the forecasts cannot be validated.
8. **Open data**: publish the measurements (for example on OpenAQ) so that researchers, media and
   citizens can use them.

## What these figures do not say

The embassy monitor measures one place, the embassy's district, and PM2.5 only. The model values
describe a cell about 44 km wide and probably underestimate exposure near major roads, dumps and
industrial zones. Recommendations 6 and 7 are the condition for going further.
